# Google Ads 广告不获批怎么办：目的地、业务身份和广告内容要分开看

本文件是网站文章的副本。正式页面：[Google Ads 广告不获批怎么办：目的地、业务身份和广告内容要分开看](https://fatevia.cn/blog/articles/google-ads-disapproved-destination-business-creative-2.html)

Google Ads 广告被拒时，原因可能出在落地页、账户业务身份或广告文案三个层面。本文说明如何从状态和政策提示判断层级，再决定修改、申诉或停止重提。

## 先看被拒的是广告、资产还是整个账户

同样是「不获批」，处理方式差别很大。在 Google Ads 后台的广告和资产列表里，先看状态列和政策详情：只有单条广告或某个素材被拒，通常是内容或目的地问题；整个广告系列无法展示，要检查是否共用了同一个有问题的最终到达网址；账户层面被暂停或要求验证，则多半和业务身份、付款或反复违规有关。不要一看到红色状态就逐条改文案，先确定被拒对象的层级，否则改了十次仍是同一个结果。

## 目的地问题：最终到达网址要能被审核方完整访问

目的地类被拒常见于网址无法访问、需要登录才能看内容、重定向到与广告不符的页面、页面缺少与所推广产品相关的实质内容，以及落地页与广告承诺不一致。判断方法是用无痕窗口、不同网络和移动端分别打开最终到达网址，确认没有地域拦截、验证码墙、证书错误或机器人拦截。同时核对页面里是否有明确的商家信息、联系方式、价格或服务条款。如果你的站点对海外 IP 或爬虫做了防护，审核抓取可能被挡在外面，这一类问题改文案没有任何作用。

## 业务身份问题：广告主是谁、卖什么要和页面一致

业务身份类问题不在单条广告里，而在账户与网站是否说得通。典型情形包括：账户资料中的公司名称、网站展示的主体和付款人信息互相对不上；网站看起来像中转站或空壳，缺少真实业务说明；推广对象属于需要额外资质或认证的类别，却没有完成对应流程；账户被要求做广告客户验证但未完成。这一层的处理重点是补齐并统一材料，而不是反复重提广告。是否需要认证、需要哪些文件，以平台当时的政策页和账户内提示为准。

## 广告内容问题：文案、素材与受限类别分开核对

内容层面的拒绝,主要看文案和素材本身是否触碰政策:夸大或无法验证的承诺、误导性表述、不规范的大小写与标点、关键词插入造成的语义错误、素材中出现受限内容,或推广品类本身属于受限、需限制展示的类别。政策详情通常会写明具体类型,按其指向修改即可。一个实用做法是只改一处再提交,这样能判断到底是哪一处触发。若同时改文案、素材和落地页,即使通过了,你也不知道真正原因,下次换账户或换产品会重复踩坑。

## 什么时候该修改、什么时候该申诉

如果政策提示指出的问题确实存在,就先修改再重新提交,不必申诉。如果你核对后认为页面和广告完全合规,例如被误判为不可访问而你能证明页面正常,才使用申诉。申诉前准备好说明:被拒的具体对象、你核对过的内容、已确认合规的依据。同一问题在没有任何改动的情况下反复提交,既不会改变结果,还可能让账户的违规记录变得难看。申诉通常也有处理时间,具体以后台提示为准,不要把它当作可以连续刷新的操作。

## 停止重提并换方案的判断条件

出现以下情况应该停下来:同一类政策在多条不同广告上重复触发,说明问题在页面或业务本身;账户收到针对业务模式的警告或暂停;推广品类需要你暂时不具备的资质。此时继续建新广告或换新账户并不稳妥,新账户若关联到同一网站、付款方式或主体,同样会带着问题。更可行的路径是先调整网站与业务说明、补齐资质或认证,再用原账户申请复审;若业务确实不在可投放范围内,就改用其他渠道,而不是绕过政策。

# Google Ads Disapproved: Separate Destination, Business Identity and Ad Content Issues

This file mirrors the site article. Official page: [Google Ads Disapproved: Separate Destination, Business Identity and Ad Content Issues](https://fatevia.cn/en/blog/articles/google-ads-disapproved-destination-business-creative-2.html)

When Google Ads rejects an ad, the cause may sit in the landing page, the advertiser's business identity or the creative itself. Learn how to locate the layer and decide whether to edit, appeal or stop.

## Start by identifying what exactly was rejected

A disapproval can hit a single ad, an asset, a whole campaign or the account. In the Google Ads interface, check the status column and the policy details. If only one ad or asset is flagged, the cause is usually content or destination. If every ad pointing to the same final URL is affected, suspect the page. If the account is suspended or asks for verification, the issue is more likely business identity, payment or repeated violations. Rewriting copy before you know the layer tends to waste several review cycles.

## Destination issues: the final URL must be fully reachable

Destination rejections typically come from unreachable URLs, login walls, redirects that end somewhere different from the ad, thin pages with little substance about the advertised product, or a mismatch between what the ad promises and what the page shows. Open the final URL in a private window, on mobile and from different networks. Look for geo-blocks, CAPTCHA walls, certificate errors and bot protection. Also confirm the page shows clear business details, contact information and terms. If your site blocks overseas IPs or crawlers, the review system may never see the page, and no amount of copy editing will help.

## Business identity: who you are and what you sell must line up

Identity problems live in the relationship between account, site and payer rather than in one ad. Common triggers include a company name in the account that does not match the site or the payment profile, a site that looks like a shell or pass-through with no real business description, promotion of a category that requires extra certification you have not completed, or an unfinished advertiser verification request. The fix is to complete and align your materials, not to resubmit ads. Whether certification applies, and which documents are needed, depends on the policy page and in-account prompts at the time.

## Ad content: check copy, assets and restricted categories separately

Content rejections concern the copy and creative themselves: exaggerated or unverifiable claims, misleading phrasing, improper capitalization or punctuation, keyword insertion that produces broken meaning, restricted material in images, or a product that falls into a restricted category. The policy detail normally names the type. Change one thing at a time and resubmit. If you edit copy, assets and landing page together and the ad passes, you will not know what actually caused the rejection, and you may repeat it on the next product or account.

## When to edit and when to appeal

If the policy note points to a real problem, fix it and resubmit; an appeal is unnecessary. Appeal only when you have verified that the ad and page comply, for example when a page flagged as unreachable loads fine for you. Prepare a short statement covering the exact item rejected, what you checked and why you believe it is compliant. Resubmitting the same unchanged content repeatedly will not change the outcome and can leave a poor violation history. Appeal handling times vary, so follow the timing shown in the account.

## Signs you should stop resubmitting and change approach

Stop when the same policy triggers across many unrelated ads, which points to the page or the business model; when the account receives a warning or suspension tied to the business itself; or when the category needs credentials you do not have yet. Creating new ads or a new account will not help if it shares the same site, payer or legal entity. First adjust the site and business description, finish certification or verification, then request review in the original account. If the business falls outside what the platform allows, use another channel rather than working around policy.
