# Google Play 与 Apple 开发者「老账号」有价值吗：账号年龄、合法转移与买卖风险

本文件是网站文章的副本。正式页面：[Google Play 与 Apple 开发者「老账号」有价值吗：账号年龄、合法转移与买卖风险](https://fatevia.cn/blog/articles/google-apple-developer-old-account-value-and-transfer.html)

搜索「老账户购买」的人，多半想绕过新账号门槛。本文说明账号年龄并非公开的审核加分项，买卖账号违反平台协议，并梳理应用转移、主体变更等合法替代路径及停止条件。

## 账号年龄不是公开的审核加分项

两家平台的开发者文档都没有说明「注册越久，审核越宽松」。审核看的是应用本身的内容、权限声明、隐私与数据披露、支付方式是否合规，以及账号主体的身份与历史违规记录。所谓老账号更容易过审，多数来自卖家的话术或个别经验，无法作为判断依据。需要提醒的是，历史记录是双向的：干净的历史不会自动加分，有过违规、退款纠纷或被关联封禁的历史，则会持续带来负面影响。

## 买来的账号，风险集中在身份与协议两处

Apple 开发者计划和 Google Play 开发者分发协议，都把账号绑定在具体的个人或法律主体上，并要求资料真实。账号背后有实名信息、付款资料、税务与收款设置，组织账号还有 D-U-N-S 编号及法人或授权人验证。这些信息不属于你，就意味着：对方可以通过邮箱、手机号或客服流程找回账号；平台复核身份时，你无法提供对应证明；一旦账号因关联而被封，上面的应用、订阅和收款余额会一起受影响。协议通常也明确限制转让账号，具体条款以平台当时的页面为准。

## 想买老账号，通常是在回避这三个门槛

一是新账号的前置要求，例如 Google Play 对新建个人账号有封闭测试等要求，Apple 组织账号需要 D-U-N-S 并经过主体验证，这些要求的细节会变，需看官方帮助页面。二是时间成本，主体验证和资料补充会拖慢上线。三是希望分散风险，把多个应用放进不同账号。这三点都有不涉及买卖的处理方式：提前准备主体资料、按要求完成测试流程，以及用合法的组织结构拆分账号，而不是借用他人身份。

## 确实需要转移时，走应用转移或主体变更

如果目标是把已有应用交给另一方，两个平台都提供应用转移功能：Apple 在 App Store Connect 里发起 App 转移，接收方须是有效的开发者账号并满足协议条件；Google Play 则在 Play Console 中把应用转给另一个开发者账号。转移前要核对应用的订阅、内购、推送证书、签名密钥和第三方 SDK 的绑定关系，这些最容易在转移后出问题。如果是公司并购或更名，应通过平台的主体信息变更或账号所有权变更流程，提交相应证明文件，而不是私下交接登录凭据。可用的选项和条件以平台当时页面为准。

## 如果对方仍然提供「老账号」，先用这份清单判断

以下任何一项无法满足，就应停止：账号主体名称与对方实际身份不一致；对方无法以主体名义配合变更邮箱、手机和付款信息；账号内已有应用或收款记录，却无法说明来源；交易要求先付款，后交接，或只提供登录密码；对方承诺「保过审」「永不封号」。这些承诺没有平台依据，交易本身也可能让你无法向平台申诉，因为你并不是协议中的合法持有人。

## 决策标准：账号是工具，主体与合规才是资产

判断是否值得投入，不看账号注册了几年，而看三件事：账号主体是否就是你的实际经营主体；应用的数据披露、权限和支付是否符合政策；出问题时你能否以主体身份与平台沟通。三项都成立，新账号和老账号差别很小。三项不成立，买到的账号越老，沉没成本越高。时间紧时，把精力放在提前完成主体验证与测试要求，通常比寻找现成账号更可控。

# Are Aged Google Play and Apple Developer Accounts Worth Having? Account Age, Legal Transfers and Resale Risk

This file mirrors the site article. Official page: [Are Aged Google Play and Apple Developer Accounts Worth Having? Account Age, Legal Transfers and Resale Risk](https://fatevia.cn/en/blog/articles/google-apple-developer-old-account-value-and-transfer.html)

People searching for aged developer accounts usually want to skip new-account hurdles. This article explains why account age is not a documented review advantage, why resale breaks platform terms, and which legitimate transfer paths exist.

## Account age is not a documented review advantage

Neither platform's developer documentation says that older accounts get easier reviews. Review looks at the app itself: content, permissions, privacy and data disclosures, payment compliance, plus the identity of the account holder and any violation history. Claims that aged accounts pass faster usually come from sellers or isolated anecdotes. History also cuts both ways. A clean record earns no automatic credit, while past violations, refund disputes or linked-account bans can keep causing problems.

## A purchased account is risky mainly because of identity and agreements

The Apple Developer Program and the Google Play distribution agreement tie an account to a specific person or legal entity and require accurate information. Behind each account sit verified identity data, payment details and tax and banking settings. Organization accounts add a D-U-N-S number and verification of the legal representative or authorized person. If that data is not yours, the original holder may recover the account through email, phone or support, you cannot prove identity during re-verification, and a linked ban takes the apps, subscriptions and payout balance with it. The agreements also restrict transferring accounts; check the platform's current terms.

## Buyers are usually trying to avoid three hurdles

First, entry requirements for new accounts. Google Play applies testing requirements to newly created personal accounts, and Apple requires a D-U-N-S number and entity verification for organizations. Details change, so check the official help pages. Second, time cost, since entity verification and follow-up requests slow launches. Third, risk spreading across accounts. None of these needs a purchase: prepare entity documents early, complete the required testing, and split accounts through a legitimate company structure rather than borrowing someone else's identity.

## When a handover is truly needed, use app transfer or entity change

To move an existing app to another party, both platforms offer app transfer. Apple handles it in App Store Connect, and the recipient must hold a valid developer account that meets the agreement conditions. Google Play lets you transfer an app to another developer account in Play Console. Before transferring, check subscriptions, in-app purchases, push certificates, signing keys and third-party SDK bindings, which break most often. For acquisitions or renamings, use the platform's entity information or account ownership change process with supporting documents instead of passing login credentials privately. Available options depend on the platform's current pages.

## If a seller still offers an aged account, run this checklist

Stop if any of these apply: the account holder's name does not match the seller's real identity; the seller cannot change the email, phone and payment details under the holder's own name; the account has apps or payout history that the seller cannot explain; payment is requested up front with only a password handed over; or the seller promises guaranteed approval or no bans. Platforms make no such guarantees, and because you would not be the legitimate party to the agreement, you would also struggle to appeal later.

## The decision rule: entity and compliance are the assets

Do not judge by how many years an account has existed. Ask three things: does the account holder match your actual operating entity, do the app's data disclosures, permissions and payments comply with policy, and can you speak to the platform as the holder if something goes wrong. If all three hold, a new account and an old one differ very little. If they do not, an older account only raises the sunk cost. When time is tight, finishing entity verification and testing requirements early is usually more controllable than hunting for a ready-made account.
