# Facebook 广告素材频繁被拒怎么办？文案、图片、落地页与政策问题排查

本文件是网站文章的副本。正式页面：[Facebook 广告素材频繁被拒怎么办？文案、图片、落地页与政策问题排查](https://fatevia.cn/blog/articles/facebook-ad-creative-repeatedly-rejected-troubleshooting.html)

Meta 广告审核失败时，先在广告质量页确认被拒的政策类别，再按文案、图片、落地页、账户层级逐层拆分变量。本文说明排查顺序、判断标准，以及何时该申诉、何时该改素材。

## 先看被拒政策类别，再决定改哪里

素材反复被拒，最常见的原因是没有读清楚拒登通知，直接凭感觉改图改字。在广告管理工具或账户质量页里，每条被拒广告都会标注所违反的政策类别，例如误导性或欺骗性内容、个人属性、受限商品与服务、不可用的落地页体验等。这个类别决定排查方向：属于文案类，改图没有意义；属于落地页类，换素材同样会再次被拒。具体类别名称和说明文字以 Meta 当时的页面为准，处理前先把被拒广告的 ID、政策类别和审核时间记录下来，后面比对时才有依据。

## 文案层：最容易触发的是对用户属性的暗示

文案被拒，高频原因通常是句式直接指向用户个人状态，比如「你是不是负债」「你的体重让你……」这类把用户属性当事实陈述的写法。另一类是绝对化承诺，如保证收益、保证通过、保证见效，在金融、健康、减重等领域尤其敏感。判断方法是：把句子里的「你」换成中性描述，如果意思不变且更像在介绍产品，就是更安全的写法。同时检查标题、主文案、描述和按钮文字是否前后矛盾，系统是把它们作为一个整体来看的，某一处夸张会拖累整条广告。

## 图片与视频层：前后对比、敏感区域与文字占比

图片类问题常见于前后对比图、身体局部特写、带有惊吓或夸张效果的画面，以及模拟系统提示、弹窗、红点通知的伪界面。后者容易被判定为误导。图中叠加大量文字也会影响投放表现，虽然这未必直接导致拒登，但会让素材更容易落入审核风险区。排查时可以把同一文案分别搭配两三张差异明显的图片提交，如果只有某一张被拒，问题就在图片本身；如果全部被拒，回到文案或落地页。每次只改一个变量，否则无法确认到底是哪一处生效。

## 落地页层：广告通过但落地页拖后腿

很多人只改广告而忽略落地页，结果换了几十版素材仍然被拒。落地页需要检查几件事：页面内容是否与广告承诺一致；是否有明确的公司或产品信息、隐私政策和联系方式；页面能否正常打开，是否存在大量跳转、强制弹窗或自动下载；域名是否曾被标记。可以用无痕窗口和手机网络分别打开页面，确认不同环境下看到的内容一致。如果广告内容和落地页内容不一致，哪怕两者各自合规，也可能被视为误导。

## 账户层：素材没问题但依然被拒的情形

如果同一素材在另一个账户可以通过，而在当前账户反复被拒，要考虑账户层面的因素：历史违规记录、支付方式异常、主页质量、账户受限状态等。这类情况单纯改素材收效有限，应先到账户质量页查看是否存在限制提示，再决定处理路径。新账户在早期审核中也可能比较严格，建议先用保守素材跑通小预算，建立稳定的审核记录。对于买来的或借用的账户，其历史状态不可见，更需要先确认账户质量，再做素材判断。

## 何时申诉，何时直接修改

申诉适合你有明确依据认为系统误判的情形，比如素材本身完全符合政策却被归入受限类别。如果素材本身存在明显触及政策的元素，申诉只会消耗时间并留下记录。一个实用标准是：同一条素材在不改动的前提下被拒两次，就不再重复提交，转入逐变量拆分。多次对同一内容提交、仅做微小改动重新上传，也可能被视为规避，反而加重账户风险。建议保留一份修改记录，写清每次改了什么、结果如何，这样即使要联系支持，也能快速说明情况。

# Facebook Ads Keep Getting Rejected: A Layered Check of Copy, Images, Landing Pages and Policy

This file mirrors the site article. Official page: [Facebook Ads Keep Getting Rejected: A Layered Check of Copy, Images, Landing Pages and Policy](https://fatevia.cn/en/blog/articles/facebook-ad-creative-repeatedly-rejected-troubleshooting.html)

When Meta ad reviews keep failing, start with the policy category shown in account quality, then isolate copy, creative, landing page and account variables one at a time. Includes when to appeal and when to rewrite.

## Read the policy category before changing anything

Repeated rejections often come from editing blindly. Each rejected ad in Ads Manager or the account quality page carries a policy category, such as misleading content, personal attributes, restricted goods and services, or a landing page problem. That category tells you where to look. If it is a copy issue, swapping the image changes nothing. If it is a landing page issue, new creative will be rejected again. Category names and wording follow Meta's current pages. Record the ad ID, category and review time first so you can compare results later.

## Copy: implied knowledge of the viewer is the usual trigger

The most frequent copy problem is phrasing that states or implies something about the viewer's personal situation, such as debt, weight or health. Absolute promises like guaranteed returns, guaranteed approval or guaranteed results are another common cause, especially in finance and health. A quick test: replace the second person with a neutral description. If the meaning survives and it reads more like a product description, it is safer. Also check that headline, primary text, description and button label do not contradict each other, since they are evaluated together.

## Images and video: before-and-after, body close-ups and fake UI

Typical image problems include before-and-after comparisons, close-ups of body parts, shocking visuals, and mock system elements such as fake alerts, pop-ups or notification dots, which are easily read as deceptive. Heavy text overlays may not cause rejection by themselves but push a creative closer to the risk zone. To locate the problem, pair one piece of copy with two or three clearly different images. If only one is rejected, the image is the cause. If all are rejected, go back to copy or landing page. Change one variable at a time.

## Landing page: the ad can be fine while the page fails

Many advertisers keep rewriting ads when the landing page is the real cause. Check whether the page matches what the ad promises, shows clear business or product information, privacy policy and contact details, loads reliably, and avoids excessive redirects, forced pop-ups or automatic downloads. Open it in an incognito window and on mobile data to confirm that different environments show the same content. An ad and page that are each compliant can still be treated as misleading if they do not match.

## Account level: when the creative is clean but rejections continue

If the same creative passes in another account but fails in this one, look at account factors: past violations, payment anomalies, page quality, or restriction status. Editing creative has limited effect here. Open the account quality page, look for restriction notices, then decide on a path. New accounts can also face stricter early reviews, so run conservative creatives at low spend to build a clean record. For accounts acquired from others, history is not visible, so verify account quality before judging creative.

## When to appeal and when to rewrite

Appeal when you have a clear basis to believe the system misclassified a compliant ad, for instance when it was placed in a restricted category by mistake. If the creative contains an obvious policy trigger, an appeal only costs time and adds records. A practical rule: if the same unchanged ad is rejected twice, stop resubmitting and split variables instead. Repeatedly uploading near-identical content with trivial tweaks can look like circumvention and raise account risk. Keep a change log of what you edited and what happened, which also helps if you need to contact support.
